Three words you'll see

Broker
A real estate agent. They help people buy, sell or rent homes, and earn when a deal closes.
Lead
Someone who might buy or rent. A broker has dozens at once, all at different stages.
CRM
Software for keeping track of leads: who they are, what they want, what happens next.

01The problem

A broker's day is scattered across apps that don't talk to each other.

Buyers message on WhatsApp. New enquiries arrive from property websites like Property Finder and Bayut. Notes live in a shared spreadsheet. The calendar fills up with viewings. Nothing connects them.

Illustrations, based on what brokers showed me.

AI SRE Lab, where I work, already sold a CRM. Brokers nodded at it in demos, then went back to WhatsApp. The CRM stayed empty.

02What I saw

I spent time with 20+ brokers at their offices.

I watched how they actually worked, then wrote down what kept coming up.

Field notesOn-site broker visits
  1. 01Their day lives in WhatsApp, calls and voice notes.
  2. 02The CRM gets nodded at in demos, then never updated.
  3. 03Their list of leads sits in Google Sheets. Several copies, too crowded to read.
  4. 04The calendar is full. Nothing says what each meeting needs.
  5. 05Ages 21 to 60. The young ones don't know the terms; the older ones get lost in the app.
  6. 06Every morning, the same three questions: What's next? Who's hot? What do I owe people?
Reconstructed for this case study
21New to the industry. Doesn't know the terms yet.
60New to the software. Knows every term, but gets lost in apps.

So every screen had to be minimal, spaced out and explained, even if that meant saying more than an expert needs.

Every morning, every broker needed answers to the same three questions.

  1. 1What's next today?
  2. 2Who is most likely to buy?
  3. 3What did I promise people?

That became the brief: answer these three before she has to ask.

03The bet

A text at 3am, and 24 hours to build.

The bet: instead of one more place for brokers to type things in, show an assistant that reads their day for them. Joel, AI SRE Lab's CEO, had a big agency coming in on Monday to see it. Nobody else on the team had sat with brokers. I had, so I designed and built the first version myself.

04What changed

The first version was wrong in one important way.

The demo was a chat box: ask it anything. But at 8am, a broker doesn't know what to ask. So I flipped it. The answers come first, and chat is there for follow-up questions.

06Trust

The AI suggests. The broker decides.

One rule, everywhere in the product: AI does the draft, the human does the deal.

We tried letting the AI send messages and publish listings by itself. It made mistakes: voice notes weren't always heard right, details were missing, and each property website has its own rules. So I added one review step before anything leaves AI SRE Pipeline.

Voice noteAI fills the fields→She ticks→Saved
WhatsApp replyAI drafts→She edits→SentAuto-send: tried, pulled back
New listingAI writes→She reviews→LiveAuto-publish: tried, pulled back
12.24%

fewer client-facing errors: mistakes reaching a client or a property website, compared with before the review step.

07Where it is today

From one demo to six agencies.

Brokers chose it over the tool they were given.

The CRM had to be pushed on brokers, and they still didn't use it. AI SRE Pipeline spread the other way: 50+ brokers created their own accounts, including some whose agencies already had it. Adoption you don't have to force is the kind that lasts.

Agencies on AI SRE PipelineOppenheim · Better Homes · Moonstay · Elite Properties · and 2 more

72% of brokers open AI SRE Pipeline first thing in the morning, before any other app. I didn't add another app to their day. I replaced the first thing they reach for.

Not working yet

The UK and Portugal.

About ten brokers so far, and too many stop using it. I'm still learning how those markets work; the redesign is only a rough sketch so far. One early lesson: the same feature needs different words in each country. Below, the name for a home valuation report, the main property websites and the currency.

What I'd do differently

I designed the first version as a chat box, and it was wrong. Now, before I design a place to ask questions, I check whether the answer should already be on the screen.
