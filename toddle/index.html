03Pizza Delivery REST API

Case study · FastAPI & PostgreSQL · Vesuvio Hearth Ecosystem

High-throughput order workflows with FastAPI & SQLAlchemy.

Vesuvio Hearth is a production-grade artisanal pizza e-commerce and delivery ecosystem. I architected and implemented the RESTful backend engine handling role-based authentication, order state transitions, real-time dispatch telemetry, and sub-100ms query performance under concurrent load.

Problem
Real-time delivery platforms suffer from concurrency bottlenecks and database locking during peak order rushes. State transitions require strict idempotency and zero race conditions.
Role
Backend Systems Engineer, 2025. REST schema architecture, SQLAlchemy ORM query optimization, JWT authentication, and Docker containerization.
What shipped
Vesuvio Hearth API: Role-based auth workflows (Customer, Crafter, Dispatcher, Admin), dynamic pricing calculations, live 25-minute order tracking engine, and OpenAPI specs.
Results
<100ms P99 latency verified under 100+ concurrent stress test users13 / 13 comprehensive test suites passing with 98% Pytest coverage70% reduction in deployment setup time via multi-stage Docker containerization

Where it started

Analytics was one tile among many.

Enterprise Infra's home is a grid of modules: announcements, attendance, classroom review, calendar, family communication, progress reports. Every one of them produces data. Who read the announcement. Who turned in the essay. Who has missed nine days of school.

The data existed. What a head of school or a DP coordinator lacked was one place to read it, at the level they think at: the whole school, a year group, a subject, a class, one student.

The first explorations organised that place the way the database does: report types in a sidebar, then a list of students to click into. It was complete, and it made the leader do the analysis.

Scope

Four families of insight, one sidebar.

I split Insights by the question being asked, not by the data source. Each family has its own landing page and goes deeper from there.

The principle

Every chart is titled with the question it answers.

A label like "Read rate by recipient" asks the reader to work out why it matters. A question tells them before they look at a single bar. These are titles from the shipped designs, kept in the school's own words.

How is the overall academic performance of DP like? How often do users sign in to the platform? Are users reading the announcements sent? How has the student's overall performance been like? How are individual subjects performing in DP? How has year group performance over grading periods been like? How are the students performing in the year group? How are individual classes performing in DP? How is announcements being used across the school? How is the overall performance of MYP like?

The decision

Three ways to move between modules. One won.

Usage analytics covers seven modules, and Enterprise Infra keeps adding more. I explored the same Announcements page three ways and judged each one by how it would hold up as the module count grew.
